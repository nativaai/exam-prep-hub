Concept & Definition

The term Percentage originates from the Latin "Per Centum", meaning "out of one hundred".

It represents a fraction whose denominator is always 100. It is denoted by the universal symbol %.

Core Rule:
• To convert Fraction to %: Multiply by 100  1/4 × 100 = 25%
• To convert % to Fraction: Divide by 100  25% = 25/100 = 1/4

Essential Exam Formulas

🔹 Basic Percentage Value
Value = (P / 100) × Total
Calculates what P% of an amount is.
📈 Percentage Increase
% Inc = (Increase / Initial) × 100
Always calculate on the original initial base.
📉 Percentage Decrease
% Dec = (Decrease / Initial) × 100
Difference divided by original starting value.
⚡ Successive % Change
Net % = a + b + (ab / 100)
Use + for increase, − for decrease.
📝

Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Ex 1 A student scores 360 marks out of 600. What is his percentage score?
Given Obtained Marks = 360, Maximum Marks = 600
Formula Percentage = (Marks Obtained / Maximum Marks) × 100
Solution (360 / 600) × 100 = 360 / 6 = 60%
Visual: 60% of Bar Filled
✓ Final Answer: 60%
Ex 2 If the price of a book rises from ₹200 to ₹250, find the percentage increase.
Given Initial Price = ₹200, Final Price = ₹250 → Increase = ₹50
Formula % Increase = (Increase / Initial Price) × 100
Solution (50 / 200) × 100 = 50 / 2 = 25%
✓ Final Answer: 25% Increase
Ex 3 The price of an item is increased by 20% and then decreased by 10%. Find the net percentage change.
Given a = +20% (increase), b = −10% (decrease)
Formula Net Change % = a + b + (ab / 100)
Solution 20 − 10 + [20 × (−10) / 100] = 10 − 2 = +8%
✓ Final Answer: 8% Net Increase

Speed-Math Tips & Fraction Shortcuts

💡
Fraction Conversion: Memorize standard fractions (1/2 = 50%, 1/3 = 33.33%, 1/4 = 25%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/9 = 11.11%).
⚡
Commutative Rule: x% of y = y% of x. Example: Calculating 64% of 50 is the same as calculating 50% of 64 (= 32).
🎯
Base Value Rule: If A is x% more than B, then B is [x / (100 + x)] × 100% less than A.
✨
Multiplier Trick: A 20% increase means multiplying by 1.20; a 15% decrease means multiplying by 0.85.
⚠️

Common Mistakes to Avoid

🚫
Mistake 1: Confusing the Base Value
Always divide percentage increase or decrease by the original initial value, never the new increased/decreased value.
🚫
Mistake 2: Assuming Reciprocal Percentages are Equal
If Salary increases by 20% and later decreases by 20%, the net value is not 0% change; it is a 4% net loss [20 - 20 - 400/100 = -4%].
🚫
Mistake 3: Direct Percentage Point Addition
Do not simply sum successive percentages (e.g., 20% discount + 10% discount is 28% total discount, not 30%).
