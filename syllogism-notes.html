Fundamental Categorical Propositions

A Syllogism deduces logical certainties from given premises, completely ignoring real-world factual associations.

4 Classical Proposition Types:
• A (Universal +ve): All A are B → A is a subset of B.
• E (Universal −ve): No A is B → Disjoint sets (A ∩ B = ∅).
• I (Particular +ve): Some A are B → At least 1 element overlaps.
• O (Particular −ve): Some A are not B → Definite exclusion of a part.
📐

Special & Modern Rules

🔹 "Only a few A are B"
Some A are B + Some A are NOT B
Definite dual meaning: Some part overlaps, and some part NEVER enters B.
🔒 "Only A are B"
All B are A (Strict Exclusive)
B can have NO relationship with any other entity except A.
⚖️ "Either-Or" Complementary Pairs
Some + No  |  All + Some Not
Both conclusions individually doubtful; same subject-predicate; one +ve & one −ve.
🔮 Possibility Rule
Definite False → Possibility False
If a statement is doubtful in basic diagram but possible in alternate, "is a possibility" is TRUE.
📝

Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Ex 1 Statements: All Pens are Books. All Books are Pens. No Book is a Marker.
Conclusions: I. No Pen is a Marker.  |  II. Some Pens are Books.
Diagram Pens ⊆ Books; Books ∩ Markers = ∅
Analysis Since all Pens reside inside Books, and no Book can touch Markers → No Pen can ever touch Markers (I follows).
Since All Pens are Books → Some Pens are Books is definitely true (II follows).
Solution Both Conclusion I and Conclusion II follow.
✓ Final Answer: Both I and II follow
Ex 2 Statements: Only a few Reds are Blues. All Blues are Greens.
Conclusions: I. All Reds can never be Blues.  |  II. All Blues being Red is a possibility.
Breakdown "Only a few Reds are Blues" means Some Reds are Blues AND Some Reds are NOT Blues.
Check I Because "Some Reds are not Blues" is definite → All Reds can NEVER be Blues (I follows).
Check II While all Reds cannot enter Blues, all Blues CAN completely enter Reds (II follows).
✓ Final Answer: Both I and II follow
Ex 3 Statements: Some Cats are Dogs. No Dog is a Cow.
Conclusions: I. Some Cats are Cows.  |  II. No Cat is a Cow.
Relation Check No direct statement connects Cats and Cows outside of the Dog overlap.
Conditions 1. Same Subject & Predicate (Cats, Cows)
2. Both conclusions individually uncertain (Doubtful)
3. Form a complementary pair ("Some" + "No")
Solution Forms a textbook complementary pair → Either I or II follows.
✓ Final Answer: Either I or II follows

Speed-Solving Tips & Shortcuts

✨
Negative Premise Law: With all positive statements (All/Some), no definite negative conclusion (No/Some Not) can ever follow.
⚡
Definite vs Possibility: If a statement is already 100% definitely TRUE in reality, its "Possibility" is considered FALSE.
🚫
All + No Exception: All A are B and No A is B do NOT form an Either-Or pair (both can be false simultaneously).
🔍
Synonyms in Exams: "Each / Every / 100%" = All; "At least / Few / Many / 1%–99%" = Some; "None / Never" = No.
⚠️

Common Mistakes to Avoid

🚫
Mistake 1: Treating "Only a few" as plain "Some"
"Only a few" includes a mandatory negative restriction. "All A being B is a possibility" is always FALSE under "Only a few A are B".
🚫
Mistake 2: Drawing Over-Specific Venn Diagrams
Always construct the Minimal Overlap (Basic) Diagram first. Never assume extra relationships not stated in the premises.
🚫
Mistake 3: Forgetting the Restricted Nature of "Only A is B"
In "Only A is B" (All B are A), entity B is exclusively reserved for A and cannot overlap or form possibilities with any 3rd entity C.
