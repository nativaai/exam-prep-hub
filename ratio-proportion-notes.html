Concept & Definition

A Ratio is a mathematical comparison of two quantities of the same kind by division. It is expressed as a : b (or a / b), where a is the antecedent and b is the consequent.

A Proportion represents an equality between two ratios. When a : b = c : d, we write a : b :: c : d.

Fundamental Rule of Proportion:
• Product of Extremes = Product of Means  a × d = b × c
📐

Essential Proportion Formulas

🔹 Mean Proportional
Mean Prop (x) = √(a × b)
For two numbers a and b, where a:x :: x:b.
📈 Third Proportional
Third Prop (c) = b² / a
For numbers a and b, where a:b :: b:c.
📉 Fourth Proportional
Fourth Prop (d) = (b × c) / a
For numbers a, b, and c, where a:b :: c:d.
⚡ Compounded Ratio
Compounded = (a × c) : (b × d)
Compound ratio of (a:b) and (c:d).
📝

Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Ex 1 Divide ₹1,500 between A and B in the ratio 2 : 3. Find each share.
Given Total Amount = ₹1,500, Ratio = 2 : 3 → Total Parts = 2 + 3 = 5 parts
Formula Share = (Individual Parts / Total Parts) × Total Amount
Solution 1 Part = 1500 / 5 = ₹300 → A = 2 × 300 = ₹600, B = 3 × 300 = ₹900
A (₹600 - 40%)
B (₹900 - 60%)
✓ Final Answer: A = ₹600, B = ₹900
Ex 2 If A : B = 3 : 4 and B : C = 8 : 9, find the combined ratio A : B : C.
Given A : B = 3 : 4  |  B : C = 8 : 9
Method Equate the common term 'B' (LCM of 4 and 8 is 8)
Solution Multiply A:B by 2 → (3×2) : (4×2) = 6 : 8. Hence, A:B:C = 6 : 8 : 9
✓ Final Answer: 6 : 8 : 9
Ex 3 Find the mean proportional between 4 and 36.
Given a = 4, b = 36
Formula Mean Proportional = √(a × b)
Solution √(4 × 36) = √144 = 12
✓ Final Answer: 12

Speed-Solving Tips & Shortcuts

💡
Bridging Rule: For A:B and B:C, A:B:C = (A×B_2) : (B_1×B_2) : (B_1×C) using the N-method.
⚡
Inverse Proportion: If speed ratio is a : b, the time taken over the same distance is in the ratio b : a.
🎯
Direct Addition/Subtraction: When equal quantities are added to both terms of a:b, the ratio moves closer to 1:1.
✨
Duplicate Ratio: Duplicate ratio of a:b is a² : b²; Sub-duplicate ratio is √a : √b.
⚠️

Common Mistakes to Avoid

🚫
Mistake 1: Comparing Quantities with Different Units
Always convert both terms to identical units before creating a ratio (e.g., ratio of 50 paise to ₹2 is 50 : 200 = 1 : 4, not 50 : 2).
🚫
Mistake 2: Confusing Third and Fourth Proportional
Third proportional requires 2 given values (b²/a), while Fourth proportional requires 3 given values (bc/a).
🚫
Mistake 3: Treating Ratio Values as Absolute Real Numbers
If boys to girls is 3:4, boys = 3x and girls = 4x. Do not treat 3 and 4 as concrete headcounts without the scaling multiplier x.
