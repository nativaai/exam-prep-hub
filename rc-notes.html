Active Reading & Passage Breakdown

Reading Comprehension (RC) evaluates your ability to digest written arguments, identify core themes, infer underlying premises, and extract specific factual details efficiently.

The 2-Phase Strategy:
• Phase 1 (Skim & Question Mapping): Read question stems first to identify keywords (names, years, technical terms), then skim the passage for structural layout.
• Phase 2 (Targeted Scan): Locate the specific paragraphs containing mapped keywords and analyze the preceding and succeeding sentences.
📐

Core Question Types & Author Tones

🎯 Central Idea / Title
Core Subject + Author's Stance
Usually found in the opening paragraph or final concluding sentences.
🔍 Direct & Fact-Based
Keyword Anchoring
Verify using exact synonyms; beware of options with partial factual distortion.
🧠 Inference & Implicit Meaning
Stated Fact → Logical Next Step
Must be 100% supported by passage evidence without bringing external bias.
🎭 Author's Tone Analysis
Descriptive / Critical / Objective
Identified by adjectives and transition words (e.g., however, alarming, promising).

Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Ex 1 Passage Snippet: "While artificial intelligence offers unprecedented automation gains, its rapid adoption without regulatory oversight poses acute risks to labor displacement and algorithmic bias."
Question: What is the primary tone of the author?
Key Clues "unprecedented gains" (positive) balanced with "without oversight poses acute risks" (cautionary)
Tone Diagnostic The author acknowledges benefits but warns against unmonitored dangers → Cautious / Critical
Solution Tone is Cautious / Prudent (neither purely pessimistic nor blindly optimistic).
✓ Final Answer: Cautious / Analytical Tone
Ex 2 Passage Snippet: "Forest cover in the Western Ghats shrank by 15% between 2000 and 2020 due to unchecked plantation expansions and road infrastructure projects."
Question: Which of the following is directly supported by the passage?
Passage Fact Forest depletion = 15%; Causes = Plantations + Roads; Period = 2000-2020.
Verification Option A: "Road projects contributed to deforestation in the Western Ghats." → Direct Match
Elimination Option B: "All forests in India will disappear by 2040." → Extreme generalization (Eliminate)
✓ Final Answer: Road projects directly contributed to deforestation

Speed-Solving Tips & Shortcuts

🚫
Extreme Word Trap: Options containing extreme absolute words like always, never, all, completely, solely are almost always incorrect in inference questions.
🔄
Pivot Words: Pay intense attention to transition signals: However, Nonetheless, Despite, Although, But, Consequently. They signal shifts in the author's primary argument.
🎯
Contextual Vocab: In vocabulary questions, replace the highlighted word with each option in the original sentence to test which fits the context best.
⚡
First & Last Paragraph Rule: In long passages, reading the introductory 3 lines and the concluding 3 lines gives you the central thesis in 80% of cases.
⚠️

Common Mistakes to Avoid

🚫
Mistake 1: Bringing External Knowledge / Bias
RC tests strict comprehension of the provided text only. Even if a statement is factually true in real life, if it is not supported by the passage, it is incorrect.
🚫
Mistake 2: Picking Options with Word-for-Word Matching but Altered Logic
Examiners craft trap choices using exact passage keywords while reversing the causative relationship (e.g., swapping cause and effect).
🚫
Mistake 3: Reading the Entire Passage Line-by-Line First
Reading a dense 600-word passage without knowing what questions are asked wastes valuable time. Always scan question stems first.
