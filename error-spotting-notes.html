🔍 Error Spotting - Study Notes

Master Subject-Verb Agreement, Correlative Conjunctions, Tense parallelism, Preposition collocations, and Conditional clauses.

✍️
💡

Grammar Scanning Framework

Error Spotting requires checking each grammatical component of a sentence systematically rather than relying purely on auditory intuition.

Order of Grammar Checks:
• 1. Subject & Verb: Do numbers (Singular/Plural) match?
• 2. Conjunctions: Are correlative pairs balanced?
• 3. Tense Consistency: Are time indicators matched to correct aspects?
• 4. Modifiers & Prepositions: Are fixed idioms and cases intact?
📐

High-Yield Grammar Rules

🔹 Proximity vs First Subject Rule
Neither...Nor → Nearest Subject
As well as / Along with → Verb agrees with 1st Subject.
🔗 Correlative Conjunction Pairs
Hardly...When | No Sooner...Than
Not only...But also | Scarcely...When | Both...And.
⏳ Conditional Clauses
If + Past Perfect → Would Have + V3
If + Simple Present → Will + V1 | If + Simple Past → Would + V1.
🔢 "One of the" Rule
One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb
If relative pronoun follows (who/that), verb becomes Plural.

Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Ex 1 Identify the error: "The captain along with his crew members (A) / were present at the harbor (B) / when the storm hit (C) / No error (D)"
Analysis Subject 1 = "The captain" (Singular), Connector = "along with", Subject 2 = "his crew members" (Plural).
Grammar Rule When subjects are joined by 'along with', 'as well as', 'with', or 'together with', the verb agrees strictly with the 1st Subject.
Correction "were present" in Part (B) must be replaced by "was present".
✓ Error in Part (B): Replace 'were' with 'was'
Ex 2 Identify the error: "No sooner had the train arrived (A) / when the passengers rushed (B) / to occupy empty seats (C) / No error (D)"
Analysis Starting phrase = "No sooner had..."
Conjunction Rule "No sooner" is always paired with "than", not "when". ("Hardly/Scarcely" takes "when").
Correction In Part (B), replace "when" with "than".
✓ Error in Part (B): Replace 'when' with 'than'
Ex 3 Identify the error: "If he would have worked hard (A) / he would have passed (B) / the examination with honors (C) / No error (D)"
Analysis Third conditional construction with conditional "if" clause.
Condition Rule Never use "would have" in the "If" conditional clause. Form: If + Subject + had + V3 ..., Subject + would have + V3 ...
Correction In Part (A), replace "If he would have worked hard" with "If he had worked hard".
✓ Error in Part (A): Replace 'would have' with 'had'

Speed-Solving Tips & Shortcuts

🔀
Inversion After Negative Adverbs: Sentences beginning with Hardly, Scarcely, Seldom, Never, No sooner take inverted verb order: Hardly had + Sub + V3.
✨
Uncountable Nouns Always Singular: Furniture, Advice, Luggage, Information, Scenery, Equipment never take plural 's' or indefinite article 'a/an'.
⚖️
Comparative Adjectives Ending in -ior: Senior, Junior, Superior, Inferior, Preferable take the preposition to, NEVER "than".
🎯
Lest + Should Rule: The conjunction lest is strictly paired with should (or bare subjunctive) and never takes a negative word like "not".
⚠️

Common Mistakes to Avoid

🚫
Mistake 1: Confusing "Between" and "Among"
Use Between for exactly 2 entities (or mutual treaties); use Among for more than 2 entities.
🚫
Mistake 2: Redundant Words in Phrases
Avoid repeating meanings: "Return back" (use return), "Cousin brother" (use cousin), "Revert back" (use revert).
🚫
Mistake 3: Misplacing "Not only... But also"
Both elements in "Not only... but also" must qualify the same part of speech (parallel structure: Verb + Verb or Noun + Noun).
