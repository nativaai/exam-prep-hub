Concept & Classification

Numbers are classified into Real Numbers (Rational & Irrational) and Imaginary Numbers.

Prime Numbers: Natural numbers greater than 1 having exactly two factors (1 and itself). 2 is the only even prime number.

Division Algorithm:
• Dividend = (Divisor × Quotient) + Remainder
• Where 0 ≤ Remainder < Divisor.
📐

Core Formulas & Divisibility Rules

🔹 Sum of First n Natural Numbers
S = [n(n + 1)] / 2
Sum of first n odd = n²; Sum of first n even = n(n + 1).
📈 HCF & LCM Product Relation
HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b
Valid for any two positive integers a and b.
⚡ Divisibility by 3, 9 & 11
3 & 9: Sum of digits | 11: Alt Diff
Divisible by 11 if (Sum of odd places − Sum of even places) = 0 or multiple of 11.
🔢 Number of Factors
N = pᵃ · qᵇ → (a + 1)(b + 1)
Where p and q are prime factors of number N.

Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Ex 1 Find the unit digit of 795 − 358.
Cyclicity Cyclicity of both 7 and 3 is 4
Powers Mod 4 95 mod 4 = 3 → Unit digit of 73 is 3
58 mod 4 = 2 → Unit digit of 32 is 9
Solution Unit digit = 3 − 9 → Borrow 10: (13 − 9) = 4
✓ Final Answer: 4
Ex 2 The HCF and LCM of two numbers are 12 and 240 respectively. If one number is 48, find the other number.
Given HCF = 12, LCM = 240, Number 1 (a) = 48
Formula a × b = HCF × LCM → b = (HCF × LCM) / a
Solution b = (12 × 240) / 48 = (2880) / 48 = 60
✓ Final Answer: 60
Ex 3 Find the remainder when (6767 + 67) is divided by 68.
Concept Remainder of (x + 1) dividing (xn) when n is odd is −1 (or x)
Remainder Form 67 ≡ −1 (mod 68) → (−1)67 + (−1) = −1 − 1 = −2
Solution Positive Remainder = 68 + (−2) = 66
✓ Final Answer: 66

Speed-Solving Tips & Shortcuts

🔁
Unit Digit Cyclicity: (0, 1, 5, 6) have cyclicity of 1; (4, 9) have cyclicity of 2; (2, 3, 7, 8) have cyclicity of 4.
⚡
Trailing Zeroes in n!: Count factors of 5 → [n/5] + [n/25] + [n/125] + ...
🎯
HCF / LCM of Fractions:
HCF = HCF(Numerators) / LCM(Denominators)
LCM = LCM(Numerators) / HCF(Denominators)
✨
Prime Verification: To test if N is prime, test divisibility only with primes ≤ √N.
⚠️

Common Mistakes to Avoid

🚫
Mistake 1: Considering 1 as a Prime Number
1 is neither prime nor composite because it has only 1 distinct factor. The smallest prime number is 2.
🚫
Mistake 2: Negative Remainder Misunderstanding
Remainders in quantitative aptitude are strictly non-negative. If calculation yields a remainder of −k, the true remainder is Divisor − k.
🚫
Mistake 3: Forgetting Power 0 when Calculating Total Factors
If prime factorization is pᵃ · qᵇ, the number of factors is (a + 1)(b + 1), not (a · b), because p⁰ is a valid factor.
