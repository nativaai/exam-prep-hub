Classical Square of Opposition (A - E - I - O)

Categorical propositions are classified by Quantity (Universal / Particular) and Quality (Affirmative / Negative).

The 4 Proposition Types:
• A: Universal Affirmative (All S are P)
• E: Universal Negative (No S are P)
• I: Particular Affirmative (Some S are P)
• O: Particular Negative (Some S are not P)
⚖️

Square of Opposition Rules & Deductive Validity

⚡ Contradictories (A ↔ O, E ↔ I)
Opposite Truth Values Always
If one is TRUE, the other is definitely FALSE. If one is FALSE, the other is definitely TRUE. They can never be true together or false together.
🤝 Contraries (A & E)
Cannot Both Be True
Universal propositions (A and E) cannot both be TRUE simultaneously, but they CAN both be FALSE at the same time.
🔄 Sub-Contraries (I & O)
Cannot Both Be False
Particular propositions (I and O) cannot both be FALSE simultaneously, but they CAN both be TRUE at the same time.
⬇️ Subalternation (A → I, E → O)
Truth Flows Down, False Flows Up
If Universal is TRUE, Particular is TRUE. If Particular is FALSE, Universal is FALSE. (Truth goes Down, Falsity goes Up).

Indian Logic: The 6 Pramanas & Nyaya Syllogism

👁️ The 6 Pramanas (Valid Means)
Pratyaksha | Anumana | Upamana | Shabda | Arthapatti | Anupalabdhi
Pratyaksha: Perception. Anumana: Inference. Upamana: Comparison/Analogy. Shabda: Verbal Testimony. Arthapatti: Postulation/Implication (Devadatta fat). Anupalabdhi: Non-apprehension (No jar on ground).
🏔️ Nyaya 5-Member Syllogism
Pratijna → Hetu → Udaharana → Upanaya → Nigamana (PHUUN)
1. Pratijna: Hill has fire. 2. Hetu: Because of smoke. 3. Udaharana: Wherever smoke, there fire (Kitchen). 4. Upanaya: Hill has smoke associated with fire. 5. Nigamana: Therefore hill has fire.
🔥 Terms of Anumana & Vyapti
Paksha (Minor) | Sadhya (Major) | Hetu (Middle)
Paksha: Subject under observation (Hill). Sadhya: Object to be proved (Fire). Hetu/Linga: Middle sign/reason (Smoke). Vyapti: Invariable concomitance between Hetu and Sadhya.
⚠️ Hetvabhasa (Fallacies of Inference)
Savyabhichara | Viruddha | Satpratipaksha | Asiddha | Badhita
Asiddha: Unproved middle (Sky-lotus). Savyabhichara: Irregular middle. Viruddha: Contradictory middle. Satpratipaksha: Counter-balanced reason. Badhita: Non-inferentially contradicted (Fire is cold).

Speed Mathematical Shortcuts (Unit 5)

🚆 Speed, Distance & Trains
Speed (m/s) = km/h × (5 / 18)
Average Speed for equal distances = $(2xy) / (x + y)$. Crossing a platform time = $(\text{Length}_{\text{train}} + \text{Length}_{\text{plat}}) / \text{Speed}$.
⏳ Time & Work
Combined Work = (A × B) / (A + B)
If A completes work in $x$ days and B in $y$ days, together they take $\frac{xy}{x+y}$ days. Use LCM method for 3-person problems.
💰 Profit, Loss & Discount
Single Equiv. Discount = D₁ + D₂ - (D₁ × D₂ / 100)
Two successive discounts of 20% and 10% = $20 + 10 - \frac{200}{100} = 28\%$. Selling Price = $\text{Cost Price} \times (100 \pm P/L)\%$.
📈 Simple & Compound Interest
2-Year CI - SI Diff = P × (R / 100)²
Direct shortcut for 2-year CI and SI difference: Difference = P × (R/100)². For 3 years: Diff = P × (R/100)² × ((300 + R)/100).

Step-by-Step Solved UGC NET PYQ Examples

Ex 1 If the statement "All philosophers are mortal" (A proposition) is given as TRUE, which of the following statements can be immediately inferred to be FALSE?
Options: (A) Some philosophers are mortal  |  (B) No philosophers are mortal  |  (C) Some philosophers are not mortal  |  (D) Both B and C
Given Proposition 'A' (All philosophers are mortal) is TRUE.
Square of Opposition Rule • Contradictory (O): "Some philosophers are not mortal" must be FALSE.
• Contrary (E): "No philosophers are mortal" must be FALSE.
• Subaltern (I): "Some philosophers are mortal" is TRUE.
Solution Both E and O statements are definitely False. Therefore, Option D is correct.
✓ Correct Answer: Option D (Both B and C are False)
Ex 2 "Fat Devadatta does not eat food during the daytime, yet he is growing steadily fatter. Therefore, he must be eating at night." According to Indian classical logic, which Pramana is illustrated here?
Options: (A) Anumana (Inference)  |  (B) Upamana (Comparison)  |  (C) Arthapatti (Implication/Postulation)  |  (D) Anupalabdhi (Non-apprehension)
Analysis We observe an apparent contradiction (fatness vs not eating by day). To reconcile these two known facts, we postulate/imply that he eats at night.
Solution Deriving an unobserved fact to explain a known inconsistency is Arthapatti (Option C).
✓ Correct Answer: Arthapatti (Postulation / Implication)

Remember EJOTY for multiples of 5 (5, 10, 15, 20, 25). Opposite letter pairs sum to 27 (A=1 + Z=26 = 27; B=2 + Y=25 = 27).

Deductive vs Inductive:
• Deductive: General → Specific (Certainty/Valid).
• Inductive: Specific → General (Probability/Strong).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

🚫
Mistake 1: Confusing Contraries with Contradictories
Contradictories (A & O, E & I) can NEVER have the same truth value (one is true, the other is false). Contraries (A & E) CAN both be false simultaneously!
🚫
Mistake 2: Mixing Up Asiddha and Badhita Hetvabhasa
In Asiddha, the middle term is unproven (e.g., "Sky-lotus is fragrant"). In Badhita, the conclusion is directly contradicted by another superior perception (e.g., "Fire is cold because it is a substance").
🚫
Mistake 3: Forgetting Unit Conversions in Speed Problems
Always convert km/h to m/s by multiplying with 5/18 whenever train lengths are given in meters and time in seconds.
