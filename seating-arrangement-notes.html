Directional Reference Framework

The primary challenge in Seating Arrangement is establishing the correct Left vs Right orientation relative to the facing direction.

Directional Orientation Rules:
• Facing Center (Inward): Clockwise = Left  |  Anti-Clockwise = Right
• Facing Outside (Outward): Clockwise = Right  |  Anti-Clockwise = Left
• Linear North-Facing: Your Left = Person's Left; Your Right = Person's Right
• Linear South-Facing: Your Left = Person's Right; Your Right = Person's Left
📐

Core Seating Layout Configurations

⭕ Circular (Inward / Outward)
n = 8 → Opposite = Pos + 4
For an even count of persons, exactly opposite person is at position (n/2).
📏 Linear Row (Single / Dual)
North vs South Rows
Dual parallel rows: Row 1 facing South and Row 2 facing North (facing each other).
⬛ Square / Rectangle Table
Corners vs Middle of Sides
Usually corners face one direction (e.g. out) and middle sides face opposite (in).
🔗 "And" vs "Who" Conjunctions
"Who" = 2nd Person | "And" = 1st Person
A is 2nd to left of B, who is 3rd to right of C → "Who" refers strictly to B.
📝

Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Ex 1 Six persons (A, B, C, D, E, F) sit in a circle facing the center. A is second to the right of B. C is sitting opposite to A. E is an immediate neighbor of both A and C. Who sits opposite to B?
Step 1 Fix B at bottom (Position 1). Since facing center, right is anti-clockwise → A sits at Position 3.
Step 2 Opposite to A (Pos 3) in a 6-seat circle is Position 6 (3 + 3 = 6) → C sits at Position 6.
Step 3 E is neighbor to both A (Pos 3) and C (Pos 6) → E is at Position 2 (or Pos 4/5 depending on direction). Opposite of B (Pos 1) is Position 4 (1 + 3 = 4) → Occupied by D/F depending on neighbors. Exactly opposite to B is E's opposite (F/D).
✓ Final Answer: D (or F by deduction)
Ex 2 5 students P, Q, R, S, T sit in a single row facing North. S is between T and Q. Q is to the immediate left of R. P is to the immediate left of T. Who is sitting in the middle?
Clue 1 P is immediate left of T → Segment: [P, T]
Clue 2 & 3 S is between T and Q → Segment: [P, T, S, Q]
Q is immediate left of R → Complete row: [P, T, S, Q, R]
Middle Person Positions: 1:P, 2:T, 3:S, 4:Q, 5:R → Middle element is S.
✓ Final Answer: S

Speed-Solving Tips & Shortcuts

🎯
Anchor with Definite Clues: Never start with vague statements like "A sits near B". Begin exclusively with fixed positional anchors (e.g., "A sits at an extreme end" or "A sits opposite B").
⚡
Parallel Possibility Diagrams: Maintain 2 parallel layout sketches (Case 1 & Case 2) side-by-side; eliminate the invalid case once a contradiction occurs.
🔄
"Immediate Left" vs "To the Left": "Immediate left" means adjacent neighbor (0 gap). "To the left" means anywhere in that leftward direction.
👥
Facing South Shortcut: When a row faces South, solve the arrangement as North-facing, then invert/mirror the entire sequence end-to-end.
⚠️

Common Mistakes to Avoid

🚫
Mistake 1: Misinterpreting Connecting Words ("And/But" vs "Who/Whom")
"A is left of B, and is right of C" → refers to A. "A is left of B, who is right of C" → refers to B.
🚫
Mistake 2: Reversing Left and Right for Outward-Facing Persons
For outward-facing seats, clockwise is Right and anti-clockwise is Left. Reversing this causes complete chart failure.
🚫
Mistake 3: Forcing a Single Diagram Without Exploring Cases
If a person can sit in 2 possible spots, immediately draw Case 1 and Case 2. Guessing on a single diagram leads to restarts and lost time.
