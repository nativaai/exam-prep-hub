Tri-Discipline Scientific Structure

General Science encompasses foundational principles and everyday real-world applications across three major domains: Physics, Chemistry, and Biology.

Core Domain Pillars:
• Physics: SI Units, Newton's Laws, Optics (Refraction/Reflection), Work, Power & Energy.
• Chemistry: Atomic Structure, Acids, Bases & Salts, Common Chemicals & Ores, Gas Laws.
• Biology: Cell Biology, Human Body Systems, Vitamins & Deficiency Diseases, Genetics.

Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Ex 1 Which physical phenomenon is primarily responsible for the twinkling of stars in the night sky?
Options: (A) Total Internal Reflection  |  (B) Atmospheric Refraction  |  (C) Dispersion of Light  |  (D) Diffraction
Physical Fact Starlight travels through different layers of Earth's atmosphere with varying optical densities and fluctuating refractive indices.
Optical Law Continuous bending of light rays through fluctuating atmospheric density layers is known as Atmospheric Refraction.
Solution Correct answer is Atmospheric Refraction (Option B).
✓ Final Answer: Atmospheric Refraction
Ex 2 Match the common commercial name with its correct chemical formula:
1. Baking Soda  |  2. Bleaching Powder  |  3. Quick Lime  |  4. Plaster of Paris
Chemical Formulations • Baking Soda: Sodium Bicarbonate ($\text{NaHCO}_3$)
• Bleaching Powder: Calcium Oxychloride ($\text{CaOCl}_2$)
• Quick Lime: Calcium Oxide ($\text{CaO}$)
• Plaster of Paris: Calcium Sulphate Hemihydrate ($\text{CaSO}_4\cdot\frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}$)

Speed-Solving Mnemonics & Shortcuts

💊
Fat-Soluble Vitamins (KEDA): Vitamins K, E, D, and A are stored in body fat and the liver. Vitamins B and C are water-soluble and excreted regularly.
🌈
Light Spectrum (VIBGYOR): Violet has the shortest wavelength and maximum deviation/scattering. Red has the longest wavelength and minimum scattering (used in danger signals).
👓
Vision Correction Mnemonic: Myopia (Short-sightedness) uses a Concave lens. Hypermetropia (Long-sightedness) uses a Convex lens.
🧪
Litmus Test (B-A-R): Blue to Red indicates Acid. Red to Blue indicates Base. Pure water has a neutral $\text{pH} = 7$; blood has $\text{pH} \approx 7.4$.
⚠️

Common Mistakes to Avoid

🚫
Mistake 1: Confusing Plaster of Paris with Gypsum
Gypsum is Calcium Sulphate Dihydrate ($\text{CaSO}_4\cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$). Heating it at 373 K yields Plaster of Paris ($\text{CaSO}_4\cdot\frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
🚫
Mistake 2: Mixing Up Mass and Weight
Mass is a scalar quantity and remains constant everywhere in the universe (kg). Weight is a vector force ($W = mg$) and changes with local gravitational acceleration (measured in Newtons).
🚫
Mistake 3: Reversing Blood Group Donation Compatibility
O Negative (O−) is the true Universal Donor (no A, B, or Rh antigens). AB Positive (AB+) is the Universal Recipient.
