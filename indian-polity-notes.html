Framing & Structure of the Constitution

The Constitution of India is the supreme law of the country, drafted by the Constituent Assembly under the chairmanship of Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (Drafting Committee).

Key Milestones & Numbers:
• Constituent Assembly Setup: Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)
• Adopted: 26th November 1949 (Constitution Day)
• Commenced: 26th January 1950 (Republic Day)
• Original: 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules
• Present: ~470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules
📐

Major Constitutional Domains

📜 Fundamental Rights (Part III)
Articles 12 to 35
Justiciable in court. Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) = "Heart and Soul" of Constitution (Ambedkar). 5 Writs.
🌱 DPSP & Fundamental Duties
Part IV (36-51) | Part IV-A (51A)
DPSP (Non-justiciable, welfare state). Fundamental Duties (42nd Amendment 1976, Swaran Singh Committee, 11 Duties).
🏛️ Parliament & Executive
Articles 52 - 151 (Part V)
President (Art 52, Head of State), PM & Cabinet (Real Executive). Lok Sabha (543) & Rajya Sabha (245, 12 Nominated).
🏛️ Independent & Statutory Bodies
ECI | CAG | UPSC | Finance Comm
Election Commission (Art 324), CAG (Art 148), UPSC (Art 315), Finance Commission (Art 280).

Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Ex 1 Which Constitutional Amendment added the words "Socialist, Secular, and Integrity" to the Preamble?
Options: (A) 44th Amendment 1978  |  (B) 42nd Amendment 1976  |  (C) 86th Amendment 2002  |  (D) 73rd Amendment 1992
Fact Check The Preamble has been amended only once in Indian history.
Amendment Rule The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 (also called the Mini-Constitution) added "Socialist", "Secular", and "Integrity".
Solution Correct option is 42nd Amendment 1976 (Option B).
✓ Final Answer: 42nd Amendment 1976
Ex 2 Match the Constitutional Writ with its literal meaning:
1. Habeas Corpus  |  2. Mandamus  |  3. Quo-Warranto  |  4. Certiorari
Writ Definitions (Art 32 / Art 226) • Habeas Corpus: "To have the body of" (Protection against illegal detention).
• Mandamus: "We Command" (Order to a public official to perform duty).
• Quo-Warranto: "By what authority?" (Check illegal usurpation of public office).
• Certiorari: "To be certified / informed" (Transfer case from lower to higher court).
✓ All 5 Constitutional Writs Memorized

Common Mistakes to Avoid

🚫
Mistake 1: Confusing Money Bill (Art 110) Rules
A Money Bill can ONLY be introduced in Lok Sabha with prior recommendation of the President. Rajya Sabha can only delay it for a maximum of 14 days; it cannot reject or amend it. Speaker of Lok Sabha decides if a bill is a Money Bill.
🚫
Mistake 2: Assuming Fundamental Rights Are Absolute
Fundamental Rights are NOT absolute; they are subject to reasonable restrictions. Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended even during a National Emergency (Art 352).
🚫
Mistake 3: Confusing Justiciable vs Non-Justiciable Provisions
Fundamental Rights (Part III) are justiciable (enforceable by courts). DPSP (Part IV) and Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A) are non-justiciable.
